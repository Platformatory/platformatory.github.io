pillars:
  - id: "api-management-platform"
    title: "API Management Platform"
    body: "The traffic plane is where cross-cutting concerns have to be solved—auth, policy, tenancy, audit, rate limits, and identity for every caller. North–south and east–west are the same plane with different trust boundaries: edge/API management and service mesh are one story, not two product silos. That was always true for APIs and service-to-service calls; it is urgent now with the explosion of MCPs and agentic sprawl, where every agent principal is another consumer on the plane."
    points:
      - "One governed traffic plane—edge to mesh—not a new perimeter per team, tool, or agent."
      - "North–south and east–west share policy and identity; only the trust boundary changes."
      - "MCP and agent access as first-class consumers, with the same policy and audit bar as human-built clients."
      - "Service mesh included in the plane—not a separate religion beside API management."
  - id: "observability-agentic-operations"
    title: "Observability & Agentic Operations Platform"
    body: "Web-scale traffic has escalated to botnet-scale—with agents in the mix. The observability platform has to take standardized telemetry across servers, services, and clients seriously, without breaking the bank. That pressure creates a specialized data domain of its own: high-cardinality, high-churn operational signal that must stay queryable, attributable, and cost-bounded while agentic operations act on it."
    points:
      - "Standardized telemetry across servers, services, and clients—one signal story."
      - "Cost-bounded by design; cardinality and retention are product decisions, not afterthoughts."
      - "Agentic operations on top of that signal—detect, decide, and act without a second shadow stack."
  - id: "internal-developer-platform"
    title: "Internal Developer Platform"
    body: "Almost nobody is coding only by hand anymore. Token-maxxing is the norm. The principles that govern internal development have shifted—code is no longer the scarce barrier—yet coding agents still have to be tamed to enterprise guardrails, domain models, and standards. The IDP has to solve the interplay between domains, context boundaries, runtimes, runbooks, and the humans who remain accountable for the software."
    points:
      - "Guardrails for coding agents: standards, domain models, and policy that survive autocomplete culture."
      - "Context boundaries and domain interplay—so agents do not freely remix what should stay separate."
      - "Runtimes and runbooks as first-class platform surfaces, not tribal memory."
      - "Humans stay in the loop as engineers of record—not spectators of an opaque agent farm."

With MCP surfaces and agentic sprawl multiplying consumers overnight, that plane is where those concerns have to land, once, and hold."
